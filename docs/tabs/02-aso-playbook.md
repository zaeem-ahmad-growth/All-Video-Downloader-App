## What works in this category

Title · 30 chars

Leaders lead with “Video Downloader” and add one differentiator: HD, private, player, story saver. A title identical to another app’s — as yours is today — splits clicks and invites confusion.

Short description · 80 chars

Name the job (save videos from a link) and the extras the title can’t hold. Your editor, MP3 extraction and vault are features almost no competitor lists.

Full description · 4,000 chars

Fully indexed. Keep “video downloader” near 2–3%, add a how-to section, and close with a copyright notice and a non-affiliation statement — both are standard in this category.

Screenshots

Your listing uses 4 of 8 phone slots. The editor and the vault are the strongest proof points and are not yet shown as framed store screenshots.

Events & offers

None of the 16 competitors runs an event or offer card in the four markets checked. A dated card (a feature launch, a trial) is uncontested promotional space.

Policy

No platform logos, no “official”, no implied endorsement. Keep platform names out of the title and short description; name supported sites only factually in the full description.

### Watch-outs

- **Platform names are high-volume and high-risk**“instagram video downloader”, “reels downloader” and similar searches carry real demand, but naming a platform in the title or short description risks removal for impersonation or trademark misuse.
- **YouTube**YouTube’s terms forbid downloading, and Play removes apps that facilitate it. YouTube keywords are excluded from this board; keep “YouTube” out of every field.
- **“No watermark” searches**Promising to strip a creator’s watermark reads as removing attribution. The keyword is tracked for evidence but not targeted.
- **“Without ads”**“video downloader without ads” scores well, but your app contains ads. A claim the listing can’t back is a misleading-claims violation.
- **Invisible audit competitors**Sky Vision, Vidow, Vidpal and the other audit apps barely rank on these keywords in any market. Their installs come from elsewhere (paid acquisition, older ranking history), so they are feature references, not keyword references.
- **English-only market data**The seven non-US markets were searched in English. Local-language boards (Portuguese, German, Spanish, Italian, Arabic, Hindi) are needed before localizing.

<a id="method"></a>

**Method, 16 Sept 2026:** Google Play web search (English) scraped live for 100 keywords in the United States and the first 40 of those in Brazil, Germany, Spain, Italy, Australia, the UAE and India — 380 result lists, 30 deep. Keywords came from a core downloader list, a platform-name list, an adjacent editing and vault list, a peripheral list and Play autocomplete; YouTube keywords and phrases with adult-site or piracy intent were removed. Full listing metadata was fetched for every app in any top 10 plus the 16 tracked competitors — 392 apps. Events & offers were read from each competitor’s listing page in four markets. Demand comes from Play autocomplete, typed out letter by letter.

**Scores:** Relevance = 0.55·intent tier (core 1, adjacent or platform-name 0.7, peripheral 0.3, off-intent 0) + 0.35·niche share of the top 10 (video downloader 1, platform-specific downloader 0.8, story or status saver 0.7, browser 0.55, video player 0.35, video editor, audio or vault 0.3) + 0.10·min(1, competitors in the top 10 ÷ 3). Opportunity = 0.35·non-brand share of the top 10 + 0.30·demand + 0.15·volume proxy (log-scaled median installs) + 0.20·entry bar (log-scaled, smaller is easier). Priority = 100 · relevance² · opportunity.

**Brands** are platform owners and established media brands identified by developer: Google, Meta (Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp), TikTok, Snap, X, Pinterest, VideoLAN, MX Media, Opera, UCWeb, Brave, Mozilla, Microsoft, Samsung, Adobe, Vimeo, Dailymotion and LinkedIn. Competitor apps are never counted as brands.
